# Lighting fundamentals

Lighting is the single most powerful lever for mood in generated images. Models trained on photography and film respond strongly to lighting vocabulary — using the right terms gets you cinematic results without having to describe physics.

## Time of day

- **Golden hour** — first/last hour of sunlight. Warm, low, long shadows, soft contrast. Romantic, nostalgic, cinematic. The most universally flattering light.
- **Blue hour** — twilight just before sunrise / just after sunset. Cool, even, glowing sky. Atmospheric, melancholic, urban.
- **High noon / harsh midday** — overhead sun, hard shadows, blown highlights. Stark, dramatic, exposed.
- **Overcast / diffused daylight** — even, soft, no shadows. Documentary, neutral, melancholy.
- **Twilight / dusk** — deep blues with residual warm sky. Mysterious, transitional.
- **Night / moonlight** — dim, cool, high contrast. Use "moonlit" for soft silver tones, "starlit" for deep cosmic dark.

## Direction

- **Front-lit** — light from behind the camera. Even, flat, friendly. Less dramatic.
- **Side-lit** — light from one side. Strong shadows, sculptural, reveals texture. Classic portrait choice.
- **Backlit / contre-jour** — light from behind the subject, into the lens. Silhouettes, rim glow, lens flare. Heroic, dreamy.
- **Rim light** — focused backlight outlining the subject's edges against a dark background. Cinematic separation.
- **Top-down** — light from directly above. Mysterious, eyes in shadow, theatrical.
- **Underlight** — light from below. Sinister, horror-movie classic, unnatural.

## Quality

- **Hard light** — small or distant source (sun, bare bulb). Sharp shadows, high contrast, defined edges. Drama, clarity.
- **Soft light** — large or diffused source (overcast sky, softbox, window with curtain). Gentle shadows, low contrast, flattering. Intimacy, calm.
- **Dappled light** — broken by leaves, lattice, or blinds. Patterned, organic, dreamy.
- **Diffused / hazy** — light scattered through fog, mist, or smoke. Atmospheric, ethereal.

## Color temperature

- **Warm** — tungsten, candlelight, fire, sunset. Orange/amber tones. Cozy, intimate, nostalgic.
- **Cool** — fluorescent, moonlight, blue hour, winter daylight. Blue/cyan tones. Clinical, lonely, futuristic.
- **Neutral / daylight** — balanced midday sun. Documentary, real.
- **Mixed temperature** — warm interior against cool exterior (window light at dusk), or vice versa. Cinematic, meaningful contrast.

## Mood / style words

- **Chiaroscuro** — extreme dark/light contrast, Renaissance painting style. Dramatic, sculptural.
- **High-key** — predominantly bright with low contrast. Light, optimistic, commercial.
- **Low-key** — predominantly dark with high contrast and small bright areas. Mysterious, noir, suspenseful.
- **Cinematic lighting** — generic but effective. Implies thoughtful three-point setup, motivated direction, and atmosphere.
- **Volumetric lighting / god rays** — visible light beams through dust, mist, or gaps. Magical, sacred, dramatic.
- **Practical lighting** — light visibly coming from a source within the scene (lamp, window, fire). Grounded, real.

## Putting it together

Stack direction + quality + color + mood for cinematic results:

> `Soft side-lit golden-hour portrait, warm rim light through her hair, shallow shadows on the wall behind, cinematic`

> `Low-key chiaroscuro portrait of an elderly blacksmith, single warm practical light from the forge, deep shadows, smoke catching the light volumetrically`

> `High-key overcast daylight, soft diffused shadows, neutral color temperature, documentary feel`
