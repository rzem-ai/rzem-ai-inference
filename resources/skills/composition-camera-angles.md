# Composition, framing, and camera vocabulary

Diffusion models have absorbed a huge amount of photography and cinematography vocabulary. Using the right shot type, angle, and lens descriptors gives you significant control over composition without having to describe it geometrically.

## Shot types (subject distance)

- **Extreme close-up** — fills the frame with a detail (an eye, a hand, a texture). High intimacy.
- **Close-up** — head and shoulders. Used for portraits, emotion, character focus.
- **Medium shot** — waist up. The conversational standard. Good for character + a hint of context.
- **Cowboy shot** — mid-thigh up. Classic Western framing, lets you see hands and posture.
- **Full shot** — entire body, head to toe, with a bit of headroom and footroom.
- **Wide shot** — subject takes up about a third of the frame. Lots of environment.
- **Establishing shot** — pure environment, no subject or tiny subject. Sets the scene.
- **Extreme wide shot** — subject is a speck in a vast landscape. Scale and isolation.

## Camera angles

- **Eye level** — neutral, conversational. The default.
- **High angle** — camera looks down at the subject. Makes the subject feel small, vulnerable, or observed.
- **Low angle** — camera looks up at the subject. Makes the subject feel powerful, looming, heroic.
- **Bird's-eye view / top-down** — straight down. Map-like, abstract, isolating.
- **Worm's-eye view** — straight up from the ground. Architectural, surreal.
- **Dutch angle / canted angle** — tilted horizon. Tension, unease, instability.
- **Over-the-shoulder** — frames a second subject from behind the first. Conversation, observation.

## Framing rules

- **Rule of thirds** — subject placed off-center on a 3×3 grid intersection. The default for "interesting" composition.
- **Centered composition / symmetrical** — subject dead center. Formal, stable, often used for portraits or architectural shots.
- **Leading lines** — roads, fences, rivers, light beams that guide the eye toward the subject.
- **Negative space** — large empty area around the subject. Loneliness, contemplation, scale.
- **Frame within a frame** — subject viewed through a window, doorway, archway, or branches.

## Lens vocabulary

- **35mm** — wide standard. Slightly distorts edges, includes lots of context. Documentary feel.
- **50mm** — "nifty fifty", close to natural human perspective. Portraits, everyday scenes.
- **85mm** — classic portrait lens. Compresses background, flatters faces, soft bokeh.
- **135mm / telephoto** — heavy compression, isolated subject, very soft background. Sports, wildlife, intimate portraits at distance.
- **Fisheye** — extreme wide-angle, curved distortion. Action sports, surreal effects.
- **Macro** — extreme close-up of small subjects. Insects, jewelry, textures.
- **Tilt-shift** — selective focus that makes scenes look like miniatures.

## Putting it together

Stack shot type + angle + lens for precise control:

> `Low-angle medium shot of a weathered fisherman repairing nets, shot on 85mm lens, shallow depth of field, harbor visible in soft background bokeh`

> `Wide establishing shot of a misty pine forest at dawn, slight high angle from a hilltop, 35mm lens, leading lines from a winding dirt path`
